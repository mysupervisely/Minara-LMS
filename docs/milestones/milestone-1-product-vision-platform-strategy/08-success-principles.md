# Success Principles

**Status:** Draft
**Milestone:** 1 — Product Vision & Platform Strategy
**Date:** 2026-08-06

These principles describe how Minara-LMS's success will be judged, at the
architecture and platform level — not specific KPIs or metrics (which
belong to a later operational milestone), but the qualities a successful
platform must exhibit.

## 1. Growth Without Redesign
Success means each new school, program, or Prepped product can be added
through configuration and content, not through re-architecture. If adding
a program requires touching core platform code, the architecture has not
met this principle.

## 2. One Learner, Coherently Represented
Success means a student's single account and profile remain coherent
across every program and school they touch — progress, grades,
certificates, and payments are never fragmented or duplicated across
systems.

## 3. Every Role Gets What It Needs, and Only What It Needs
Success means each portal exposes exactly the capability and data
appropriate to its role — no role is blocked from what it legitimately
needs, and no role can reach what it should not (RBAC working as
intended).

## 4. Trustworthy Records
Success means academic, financial, and clinical/externship records are
accurate, auditable, and defensible — a requirement rooted in
accreditation readiness and institutional accountability, not just data
correctness.

## 5. Accessible and Reachable
Success means the platform is genuinely usable on mobile devices and by
learners with a range of abilities — not usable in principle, but usable
in practice, for the actual population Minara serves.

## 6. Secure by Default
Success means security is never something a program or portal has to
opt into — it is the default posture of the platform for every user and
every data type.

## 7. AI That Helps Without Overreaching
Success means the AI learning assistant demonstrably helps students learn
while never making unsupervised decisions of academic or clinical
consequence — human-reviewed workflows are respected in practice, not
just in policy.

## 8. Operationally Legible
Success means institutional administrators, program directors, and
accreditation reviewers can understand what happened on the platform —
who did what, when — without needing engineering support to reconstruct
events.

## 9. Sustainable to Extend
Success means the platform's modular boundaries hold up over time: new
capabilities can be added by future teams without deep knowledge of
unrelated modules, and without destabilizing them.

## ⚠️ Needs Verification
- Concrete, measurable success metrics (e.g., specific uptime targets,
  specific accessibility conformance level such as WCAG 2.1 AA, specific
  performance budgets) are intentionally not specified in this
  architecture-only milestone and should be defined in a later,
  more technical milestone once relevant standards are confirmed with
  stakeholders.
